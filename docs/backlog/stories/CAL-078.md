## Context

EPIC-06 (recurring events; its goal opens "Birthdays repeat yearly"). Filed
from PO-1 in CAL-077's `## Notes` (branch
`story/CAL-077-a-yearly-series-moved-across-the-end-of`,
`docs/backlog/stories/CAL-077.md`, "PO-1"), where the defect was first
recorded and deliberately not absorbed.

The defect, reproduced at PLANNED with the real `expand` (`## Notes`,
"Reproduction"): a one-day all-day yearly event on 28 February is drawn on
two days, 28 and 29 February, in every leap year. `expand`
(`shared/recurrence.ts:163`) builds a later occurrence's end by moving the
series row's END to the same month and day of the target year. The row of a
28 February birthday ends at midnight on 1 March, so in 2028 the occurrence
runs 28 February 00:00 to 1 March 00:00: 48 hours. The month grid
(`eventsOnDay`, `src/calendar/MonthView.tsx:67`) then puts a chip in both
cells, both with the key `<id>@2028-02-28`. The same occurrence is returned
for a range that opens at midnight on 29 February, so the day view of
29 February shows it as well.

The rule this story puts in its place (PO, PLANNED, 2026-11-20; see "Decided by the
user" in `## Notes` for the two consequences that go beyond the
one-day case): **an occurrence keeps the series' own length, measured on the
wall clock of `recurrence.tz`** - calendar days for an all-day event - and
not in elapsed time, and not by re-anchoring the end on the row's end date.
Its end is its own start's wall time plus the wall-clock distance between
the row's start and end. For every daily and weekly occurrence, and every
yearly one whose span does not meet a 29 February, that is the instant
`expand` returns today.

The module is shared with the reminder function (`architecture.md`,
"Recurrence": one implementation, two runtimes), so the fixture cases run
under Deno too.

Required gates that fail if this breaks: `unit` and `coverage` (both run
`shared/recurrence.test.ts` and `src/calendar/MonthView.test.tsx`) and
`deno-test` (runs the shared fixture). None is optional. Re-read against
`bash scripts/gates.sh --list` on 2026-11-20 (`main` at `95cac12`): `lint`,
`typecheck` and `build` are required too and list `shared/**` and `src/**`
under `covers`; `deno-check` (required) type-checks `shared/recurrence.ts`

**Block 2 - the rule, and what each number means.** For occurrence `k >= 1`:

    end = fromWall(sWall + (endWall - startWall), f);

where `sWall` is the occurrence's start as a wall time in `recurrence.tz`
(already computed at `shared/recurrence.ts:151`, and narrowed to a number
by the `continue` on `:152`), and `endWall - startWall`
is the series row's length on that zone's wall clock (`:141-142`). `Wall` is
`number` (`:46`), so the line typechecks as written. It replaces line 163,
`end = fromWall(shift(endWall, freq, k, false) as Wall, f);` (still line
163 on `main` at `95cac12`). Nothing else in
`expand` changes: occurrence 0 is still the row byte for byte, the start is
still `fromWall(sWall, f)`, a 29 February start is still skipped in a year
without one, the range test and the overrides are as they are.

- *Wall clock, not elapsed.* An all-day event must end at local midnight. On
  the day the clocks go forward, one calendar day is 23 hours; adding the
  row's 24 elapsed hours ends at 01:00 the next day and draws a second chip
  (AC-4a's control). Calendar days is also how the app measures an all-day
  span elsewhere: `differenceInCalendarDays(endsAt, startsAt)`,
  `src/domain/moveEvent.ts:25`.
- *Not re-anchored.* Moving the row's end to "the same month and day, k
  years on" is the defect: the distance from 28 February to 1 March is one
  day in 2026 and two in 2028.
- *Where the two rules agree.* `Date.UTC` day arithmetic is linear, so for
  `daily` and `weekly` the two give the same wall time always, and for
  `yearly` they differ only when a 29 February lies inside the span in one
  of the two years and not the other. Measured: on the eleven series of the
  Reproduction the rule changes five lines, and they are AC-1's case (2028
  and 2032), AC-3a, AC-3b and AC-3c; every other line is identical
  (`## Notes`, "The rule on a scratch copy").

If GREEN writes it differently the result must be the same instants; the
line above is pinned so that DV-1's expression can be written now. After the
change `shift` is never called with `strict` false. Whether GREEN removes the
parameter and its comment (`:111-113`) is GREEN's choice; no test pins it.
The comment at `:162` must say what the line now does.

### Decided by the user, 2026-11-20: keep the length

The rule "an occurrence keeps the series' length" fixes the 28 February
birthday and necessarily decides two neighbouring cases. Both were put to
the user at PLANNED, pinned in AC-3 as the rule gives them. The user's
answer, relayed by the orchestrating session on 2026-11-20: "Keep the
length". Both cases stand as drafted, and AC-3 is unchanged.

1. A three-day event 27 February to 1 March 2026 is, in 2028, 27 to 29
   February (three days). Today it is 27 February to 1 March (four days).
   The rule keeps the length, not the last date.
2. A two-day event first saved in a leap year on 28 and 29 February 2028 is,
   in 2029, 28 February and 1 March (two days). Today it is 28 February
   only.

A timed event from 23:00 on 28 February to 01:00 on 1 March is two hours in
2028 instead of 26 (AC-3b); that one is the same defect and is not a
question.

### Decision: wall-clock length, not elapsed time

An all-day event is stored as local midnight to local midnight
(`architecture.md`, Data model), and a day is not always 24 hours. Keeping
the row's elapsed milliseconds would end a one-day event at 01:00 the next
morning on the day the clocks go forward, and the grid would draw a second
chip: the same picture as this defect, from the other direction. Keeping the
wall-clock length ends it at local midnight. The case in reach that tells
the two apart is AC-4a (28 March 2027 in London, 23 hours); it passes today
and fails under the elapsed rule (measured below). CAL-023 Block 3 already
chose wall time over elapsed time for the same reason; this story keeps
that and changes only what the end is measured FROM (the occurrence's own
start, not the row's end date).
